## Market research {#market-research}

Label: Market research
Home: yes
Summary: Research into customer demand, local competition, and realistic opportunities for growth, before I recommend a website or a marketing plan.

Before I recommend a website, a page, or a marketing plan, I want to know how your customers actually look for what you sell. Good recommendations come from evidence: what people search for, who they find, and what those businesses offer that you don't, or the other way around.

**Customer demand.** The searches and questions behind what you sell, in the words customers use, and how they differ by service and by part of town.

**Local competition.** Which businesses show up in Google Search and Google Maps for those searches in your area, what their websites and Google listings do well, and what they leave out.

**Your starting point.** Your current website and Google Business Profile, your reviews, and, if you have it, the data Google already collects for you in Search Console and your profile's performance report.

### How customers decide {#messaging}

Research also covers the conversation you have with customers every day: how your business earns trust, what people ask before they buy, and what you'd tell them across the counter. That becomes the plain-language copy and the pages a website needs.

Research shows where opportunity is likely. It can't guarantee results, and when the evidence is thin, I'll say so.

::: gets What you get
- A plain-English summary of how customers search for what you offer
- A look at the local competitors who show up instead, and how they present themselves
- A short, prioritized list of realistic opportunities, including the ones not worth chasing
- A recommended site map and messaging, if a website is part of the plan
:::

::: when When it's useful
- Before you build or redesign a website
- You're adding a service or moving into a new area
- Competitors show up on Google for work you do better, and you're not sure why
- You're starting a new business and want to know what customers look for
:::

## Website development {#website-development}

Label: Website development
Home: yes
Summary: A fast, distinctive, professionally designed website built around the research: the pages customers look for, in the words they use.

The research decides what the website needs to do: which services get their own pages, which questions the pages answer, which areas you serve, and the clearest path from "just looking" to getting in touch. Then I design and build it.

### Design around your business {#web-design}

Your website shouldn't look like your competitor's with the logo swapped. A pediatrician's site should feel calm and trustworthy. A heating and air company's site should put the phone number where a stressed homeowner can reach it with one thumb. A family skincare brand needs room for its story and its products.

I'll explain every decision as we go: why the layout is ordered the way it is, why I chose a typeface, which photos go where. Most of your visitors will be on a phone, so I design the phone version as carefully as the desktop one, and I build the site to load fast. Photography matters more than almost anything else, so I plan every photo slot with you and can help coordinate the shoot.

### Care after launch {#care}

A website isn't finished at launch. Hours change, services change, you finish a project worth showing. Care can include keeping an eye on hosting and your domain, making text and photo updates, adding pages, and keeping your Google Business Profile in step with the website.

::: gets What you get
- A custom design for desktop, tablet, and phone, built on the research
- Page-by-page copy written in your voice, for your review
- A working preview you can open on your phone and share
- A photo plan with sizes, crops, and shot descriptions
- A fast, accessible site, with optional care after launch
:::

::: when When it's useful
- Your site looks dated or doesn't match the quality of your work
- It's hard to use on a phone, or customers can't find what they need
- You're launching a new business or a new service
:::

## Search marketing {#search-marketing}

Label: Search marketing
Home: yes
Summary: Ongoing work to improve how you show up in Google Search and Google Maps: research, new content, website improvements, and your Google Business Profile.

Showing up in search isn't a one-time setup. Customers' questions change, competitors change, and Google changes. Search marketing is the ongoing work of finding where you could be more visible and doing something about it.

### Website SEO {#website-seo}

For a local business, that usually means one clear page for each service you offer, pages for the areas you actually serve, and helpful articles that answer the questions customers ask before they call. I connect related pages so visitors and search engines can follow the thread, from a problem, to the service that fixes it, to the place you serve. Titles, descriptions, headings, structured data, and a sitemap are handled as part of the build, not added later.

### Google Business Profile {#google-business-profile}

For many local businesses, the Google Business Profile is the first thing a customer sees: the listing with your hours, reviews, photos, and the Call and Directions buttons on Google Search and Maps. I'll help you get it right and keep it that way:

- **Categories and services.** The primary category that best describes your business, secondary categories only where they fit, and your services in the words customers use.
- **Accurate business details.** Name, phone, website, hours, and service areas that match your website and what you actually do.
- **Photos.** A plan for the photos that help people choose you.
- **Verification guidance.** Google decides how and whether a profile is verified. I'll walk you through the steps and help you prepare.
- **Reviews.** A simple routine for asking happy customers for a review and responding to every review in a way that helps the next customer decide.

### Ongoing content and improvements {#content}

As the research points to new opportunities, I add pages and articles, improve the pages that aren't doing their job, and check the results in Search Console and your Business Profile's performance report before deciding what to do next.

I can't promise rankings, verification, or a number of calls. Google controls those. What I can do is research where the opportunities are, do the work well, and tell you plainly what the data shows.

::: gets What you get
- A page plan for your services and service areas
- A complete, accurate Google Business Profile with a photo and review routine
- New pages and articles, written with your input, where the research shows demand
- Regular check-ins on what the data shows and what to try next
:::

::: when When it's useful
- People can't find you when they search for what you do in your area
- Your Google listing has wrong details, few photos, or few reviews
- You're adding services or expanding into new areas
:::
