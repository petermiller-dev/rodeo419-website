03Process

Five stages, with the research before the website

You'll always know what's happening, what I need from you, and what comes next. Research comes before any design, so the website and the search work are built on how your customers actually look for what you sell.

  1. 01

    Discover

    Understand your business, your customers, and what you want to achieve.

  2. 02

    Research

    Investigate how customers search for what you offer, and which competitors they find.

  3. 03

    Build

    Develop the website around the research, review it together, and launch.

  4. 04

    Grow

    Pursue the opportunities the research found, to increase your visibility.

  5. 05

    Measure

    Track how people find the business, and identify what to improve next.

01

Discover

We start with a conversation, by phone or in person around Fort Worth. I'll ask what you do, who your best customers are, how they usually find you, and what you'd like the next year to look like. If you have a website or a Google Business Profile, we'll look at them together.

By the end, you'll know whether I'm the right fit and what I'd suggest researching first. If I'm not the right fit, I'll tell you.

Where you come in

Tell me about your business, your best customers, and what's working or frustrating. Share your current website and Google listing if you have them.

02

Research

This is where the plan comes from. I look at the searches and questions behind what you sell, which businesses show up in Google Search and Maps for them in your area, and what their websites and listings do well or leave out. I also review your own website, Google profile, and reviews.

You'll get a plain-English summary: what customers look for, where the competition is strong, where there's realistic room to grow, and what I'd recommend first. Research shows where opportunity is likely, not a guaranteed result, and I'll tell you when the evidence is thin.

Where you come in

Answer questions about your services, prices, and service area. Share reviews, common customer questions, and access to Search Console or your Business Profile if you have them.

03

Build

The research decides the pages: the services people search for, the questions they ask, and the areas you serve. I write the copy and design the pages together, because each one shapes the other, and you get a working preview you can open on your phone, not a static mockup.

We go through the preview together with a short checklist of the details only you can confirm, and I refine it until it's right. When you approve, I connect your domain, submit the sitemap, make sure your Google Business Profile points to the new site, and test everything again once it's live.

Where you come in

Review drafts and the working preview. Check facts, prices, hours, and anything with your name on it. Help with photos, and approve the launch.

04

Grow

After launch, the search work continues at a pace that fits your business and budget: new service and area pages where there's demand, articles that answer customers' questions, a Google Business Profile that stays accurate and active, and improvements to pages that aren't doing their job. Care for the site itself, such as updates, hosting oversight, and small changes, can be part of this stage.

Where you come in

Send news, new work, photos, and the questions customers ask. Ask happy customers for reviews.

05

Measure

Two free Google tools do much of the measuring, and neither needs a tracking script on your website. Search Console shows which searches your pages appear for and how often people click. Your Business Profile's performance report shows calls, direction requests, and website visits from your listing. Together with what customers tell you, that shows what's working.

On a regular schedule, we look at the numbers, decide what to keep doing, and choose the next improvement. I'll report what the data shows, including when something didn't work.

Where you come in

Tell me what you hear from customers: how they found you and what they asked. Keep Search Console and your Business Profile in your own name.

A better foundation

Built for your business. Not a platform.

Most small businesses don't need complicated website software. They need a fast, dependable website that represents their company professionally, helps customers find them, and turns visitors into inquiries.

That's why I take a different approach.

Less complexity. Fewer things to break.

Every unnecessary layer of software introduces another potential maintenance issue. By building directly on established web standards, I eliminate much of the complexity that traditional website systems require.

Performance by design.

Speed shouldn't be an afterthought. Every website is built with performance in mind, from its underlying structure to its typography, imagery, and animations.

Built to be found.

Search engine optimization begins with the foundation of your website. Page structure, internal linking, accessibility, and content organization are considered from the beginning.

Your business. Your website.

Your website should be a business asset, not something you are permanently renting from a technology company. Portable source code gives you greater flexibility over where and how your website is hosted.

Designed for the years ahead.

As your business grows, your website should grow with it. New services, locations, educational resources, and marketing campaigns can be added without rebuilding the entire foundation.
